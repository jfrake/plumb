# Editor checklist

Concrete checks for the editor reader's five questions. Sources: Daft (1985) on why manuscripts are rejected; the Academy of Management Journal "Publishing in AMJ" series; Academy of Management Review statements on first-round rejection reasons; Suddaby (2010) on construct clarity; Alvesson and Sandberg (2011) on problematization; and a coding of the points referees raised on pilot papers. The checks are general; none quotes a referee or names a paper. Items marked (framing) repeat a framing pack check. A pack note tells you when to skip them.

Each item carries a stable id in square brackets, such as [1a]. The editor reader answers every id once in the Checks section of its output.

## 1. Conversation and lineage

- [1a] State in one sentence the conversation the paper joins: the question, and the works that framed it. If the paper itself never says, that is a finding.
- [1b] If the paper could be read as joining two or more conversations, say which, and what each reading would ask of it.
- [1c] Ask whether the argument would be stronger in a different conversation than the one the paper chose. If so, name it and say what the paper's own material would contribute there.
- [1d] First, before the classics list, check the paper's own reference list for foundational works on its topic. Check whether the paper cites a foundational work only in passing while its argument depends on that work. When you say the paper cites a work only in passing, list every page where it cites it.
- [1e] Then check the classics list in `stage-2/cocitation.md` for works relevant to the argument and are absent from the paper. A work that many of the paper's own sources build on is the strongest candidate.
- [1f] Before calling a work absent, search the reference list and the text for its authors and for the topic's key terms. If the paper cites related work, say how it uses it. Before a main point depends on a work, check whether the paper cites it; if it does, start from how the paper uses it.
- [1g] Check each candidate's date against the "Search cap:" line in `{WORKSPACE}/stage-2/cocitation.md`, or against the paper's own date when the paper shows one and it is earlier. A work dated within about a year before that date is concurrent: name it as related work, never as prior work the paper ignored.
- [1h] If the paper already draws on many literatures, or you find it lacks focus, ask which conversation is its home. The limit applies only to suggestions of further literatures for the paper to engage: across all findings, suggest at most one or two further works, the most central to that home. Works used to define or check a construct, to test a premise, or as evidence for a point do not count toward the limit.
- [1i] Always state, as its own line in the output and even when you raise no finding here, which conversation is the paper's home and whether another conversation would serve the argument better, with the reason in one sentence.
- [1j] (framing) Check whether the framing claims more agency, intent, or generality than the findings show, for example a framing about actors' strategy for findings that show an outcome no one intended.

## 2. Premise

- [2a] Find the sentence that states the view the paper argues against. Search for whether the field holds it. If the field already says otherwise, say so with a source.
- [2b] Check whether the finding the paper calls surprising is already known in an older or neighboring literature.
- [2c] For each source the paper cites for its premise, check that it says what the paper says it does. Before saying it does not, quote the whole relevant passage, including any sentence that qualifies or contradicts the point, with no ellipsis that drops a sentence relevant to the point. If the source says more than one thing, say so and weigh it.
- [2d] (framing) Check whether the motivation is only a gap, or shows why filling it changes something: an assumption overturned, a debate settled, a puzzle explained.

## 3. Constructs

- [3a] For each central construct, find its definition. A construct used without one, or used before it is defined, is a finding.
- [3b] Check that the construct keeps one meaning across the paper. Track the theory used to justify it; a justification that shifts between sections is a finding.
- [3c] Check that the construct is distinct from its neighbors, and say which neighbor it is closest to.
- [3d] Check that each measure captures the construct as defined, and not a related one.

## 4. Setting

- [4a] List the facts about the setting the argument relies on (who decides, how often, under what rules) and check each against `stage-2/setting.md` or a search.
- [4b] Ask whether someone who works in the setting would find the scenario or the materials realistic: the decisions described, the numbers used, the information people would have.
- [4c] Ask which actors in the setting would react differently, given the paper's own mechanism, and whether the paper says so.
- [4d] Ask what institutional features of the setting (contracts, regulation, professional norms) could change the result.
- [4e] Say where the argument should stop applying, and whether the paper states that boundary.

## 5. Stakes

- [5a] Say who outside the paper's subfield would change a belief or a decision if the paper is right.
- [5b] Check whether the practical implications follow from the evidence or restate the theory.
- [5c] (framing) Check whether the paper states its core point in one sentence.
- [5d] Check whether the paper tries to do too much or too little for one paper.
- [5e] Write the stakes as an argument the authors must make, never as a rating.

## Before you write

- If any finding says the paper is too broad or lacks focus, cut the works suggested elsewhere to the one or two most central.
- Mark a finding "Weakens a claim" only if, to answer it, the authors would have to change a main claim or the evidence or argument for it. Mark it "Undermines the main claim" only if, when right, it makes the main claim untenable. Mark the rest Minor.
- Sort the numbered findings from most to least consequential; put the one-line lines for questions with no finding after the list.
- Answer every id above exactly once in the Checks section.
