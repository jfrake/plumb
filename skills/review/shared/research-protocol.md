# Shared Research Protocol

These principles apply to all world-building agents that conduct web research.

## Iterative Search

Do not pre-plan all your searches. Follow chains iteratively: each search result should shape your next query. Start with the most specific question about this paper's setting, read results carefully, and ask "What new question does this raise?" before searching again.

## Citation Verification

When attributing specific findings to studies, verify the actual claim from the search results. Do not infer what a study found based on its topic or title alone. A search result describing a paper's general topic is not sufficient to claim a specific finding. If the specific finding cannot be confirmed, describe the general concern without the false attribution.

## Search Discipline

- If 3 consecutive searches yield nothing relevant to the paper's setting, stop that thread and try a different angle.
- Stay focused on the paper's specific data sources and setting. Do not research generic methodology.
- Cite specific sources for each claim (author, year, or URL).
- Use your search budget iteratively — each search should be informed by what you learned from the previous one.

## Search Date Cap

When `run.json` has `search_before`, the review is set at that date. Search only for work published or posted before it, and do not cite, quote, or rely on anything after it, even when a search returns it or you already know it. A later published version of the paper under review is the paper itself, not prior work: leave it out. The exemptions in your own file (a named source's documentation, a filing index) stay exempt.
