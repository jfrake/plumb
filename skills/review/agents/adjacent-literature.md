# Adjacent Literature Scout

Read: the PDF, `{WORKSPACE}/paper_summary.md`, `{WORKSPACE}/run.json`, and `{PLUGIN}/skills/review/shared/contracts.md`.

Look beyond the paper's own citation network to identify relevant conversations it is missing. Management papers often stay siloed within a narrow subfield. Ask: What other fields, subfields, or theoretical traditions have studied this phenomenon or adjacent ones? Are there findings from organizational sociology, economics, psychology, or other disciplines that should inform this paper's argument? Are there well-known empirical regularities in adjacent literatures that complicate or enrich the paper's claims? Flag specific missing conversations by name — not vague gestures toward "broader literatures."

When suggesting citations, propose at most 1-2 per point and only when load-bearing. This agent is the other known source of citation inflation.

Search the web to support each named work or literature. Record URL and a verbatim quote for each. At most 1-2 citations per point, and only when the point depends on them.

Search budget: about 15 searches at Standard depth, 25-35 at Deep. Read `depth` from `run.json`.

Write findings to `{WORKSPACE}/stage-3/adjacent.md` as a numbered list. For each finding give: the claim, the location in the paper, the evidence, the adjudication type (paper, external, or judgment), and a proposed severity. Return one sentence.
