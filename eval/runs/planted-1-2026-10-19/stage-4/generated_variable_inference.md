# Inference with generated variables

Scope note. No generated variable is a main treatment or main outcome. The treatment (backing ratio, BR) is human-coded (pp. 13-14) and the outcome is the log change in the VIX (p. 13). The generated variables are two moderators that carry H2 and H3 (speech positive tone, business media fear) and six controls (fingerprint, `Generated variables:`). Every one is a fixed word list, a vendor index, or a readability formula. None comes from a trained classifier, topic model, embedding, or LLM (`stage-2/model_provenance.md`). The checks below are scaled to that.

## Issues
1. Business media fear, the H3 moderator, is averaged over a window that includes the day after the speech, so it can contain the media's reaction to the speech and to the same day's VIX move. The interaction then multiplies BR by a quantity partly caused by the speech or by the outcome.
   Check: C13. Variable: Business media fear (moderator or mechanism). Tag: [paper]
   Paper's best case: The author wants the emotional climate in which traders read the speech, and a centered three-day window is a common way to smooth a noisy daily index.
   Evidence: Window defined as t-1 to t+1 (p. 16). The outcome window is t-1 to t0 (p. 13). So the fear measure's t0 and t+1 days overlap with and follow the outcome day. Fear words in t+1 business news ("worrisome, concerning, anxious, fearful, panicky," pp. 15-16) can describe the speech itself or the speech-day market move. The only window robustness reported is "a two- and four-day window for the business media fear variable" (p. 25), with no statement of where those windows start, and no estimates shown ("My findings remained consistent," p. 25). The same post-speech fear measure also defines the N = 48 crisis subsample that "entirely reversed" the main effect (p. 21). Table 2 Models 4-5 give BR x fear = 30.590 (12.626) and 26.293 (12.482) (p. 56). The mechanism-discrimination file raises the same window from the standpoint of rival mechanisms (`stage-4/mechanism_discrimination_issues.md`); this entry is the measurement-timing side of it, and the compiler may merge the two.
   Excerpt: "I constructed a business media fear variable by taking the average of this fear index over a three-day window (t-1 to t+1) around the day of the speech."
   Page: 16
   Severity: Weakens a claim
   What would settle it: Rebuild fear from days strictly before the speech (for example t-3 to t-1, or the close of t-1 for after-close speeches), re-estimate Models 4-5 and the p. 21 subsample with it, and report the estimates for each window the paper tried, with each window's start and end dates.

2. Speech positive tone, the H2 moderator, is a LIWC count over the whole speech, including the backing paragraphs that define BR. So tone and BR are computed from the same text, and the BR x tone interaction cannot be separated from what the non-backing paragraphs say about the economy.
   Check: C13. Variable: Speech positive tone (moderator or mechanism). Tag: [paper, judgment]
   Paper's best case: Tone is meant as a feature of the whole speech that frames how listeners read the backing, so computing it over the whole speech matches the construct.
   Evidence: Tone is "the percentage of words psychometrically related to positive emotion ... in relation to all words in a speech" (p. 15). The example words include "good," "positive," "great," "favorable" (p. 15). In policy speech these words often describe economic conditions. The non-backing paragraphs are, by the coding rule, the ones that use "data or warrants to make claims," most often "about the state of the economy" (p. 14). A speech with low BR therefore has more outlook text available to score as positive, and the tone score partly measures how good the reported economic news is. The paper does not compute tone on the non-backing paragraphs only, or on the backing paragraphs only, and does not control for the economic content of the outlook paragraphs. Stage 3 kept "crowded-out outlook information" as an alternative to investigate (`stage-3/alternatives.md`). Interaction estimates: -0.056 (0.020), Model 3; -0.049 (0.020), Model 5 (p. 56).
   Excerpt: "Positive emotional content is captured using a dictionary approach, whereby the percentage of words psychometrically related to positive emotion (e.g., happy, good, nice, positive, great, favorable, etc.) are reported in relation to all words in a speech."
   Page: 15
   Severity: Weakens a claim
   What would settle it: Compute tone separately on backing and non-backing paragraphs and re-estimate Models 3 and 5 with each. Alternatively, drop economic-sense words ("good," "favorable," "strong," "growth" and the like) from the positive list and report the interaction under both lists.

3. Neither moderator's prediction error is corrected or bounded, and the H2 moderator is not reported across alternative dictionaries or scorings, yet the paper states effect magnitudes at plus and minus one SD of each moderator.
   Check: C2, C4. Variable: Speech positive tone (moderator or mechanism); Business media fear (moderator or mechanism). Tag: [paper]
   Paper's best case: Both are published or vendor dictionary scores used as built. Classical error in a moderator would shrink the interaction, which would make the reported interactions conservative.
   Evidence: No correction (validation subsample, SIMEX, bound, or error-in-variables model) appears in the Methods (pp. 15-17) or Robustness (pp. 24-25). The paper never argues the error is classical. For tone it is unlikely to be: the economic sense of LIWC positive words (Issue 2; `stage-2/model_provenance.md`, LIWC entry) gives an error that moves with outlook content and so with BR. For tone (C4), no alternative dictionary (for example the Loughran-McDonald positive list, which the paper already uses for uncertainty, p. 17), LIWC version, or scoring is reported. For fear (C4), the only variant is the window (p. 25), reported as "remained consistent" with no coefficients. The magnitude statements rest on the uncorrected scores: "increasing the expected range by 3.5 percent" at -1 SD tone (p. 19), and "amplified by 2.5 percent" at +1 SD fear (p. 19). For tone both C2 and C4 are missing. For fear C4 is present but thin (windows only, no estimates shown).
   Excerpt: "Interestingly, when a speech’s positive tone is one standard deviation below its mean, this actually amplifies market uncertainty, increasing the expected range by 3.5 percent."
   Page: 19
   Severity: Weakens a claim (positive tone); Minor (business media fear, where window variants count as a thin C4)
   What would settle it: Report Models 3 and 5 with tone scored by at least one other positive-word list and by the LIWC version actually run, as a table of coefficients. Report the fear-window variants as coefficients, not a sentence. Either bound the +/-1 SD magnitudes for measurement error, or drop the magnitude statements and keep the sign claims.

4. The moderator inputs cannot be reproduced from what the paper discloses. The LIWC version is not named, and the cited LIWC2007 has no "power" category. The TRMI feed, version, and extraction date are not given, and the 1998 to mid-2012 values must be a vendor backfill. The "partial" vagueness dictionary is not specified. No scored outputs are said to be archived.
   Check: C3. Variable: Speech positive tone (moderator or mechanism); Business media fear (moderator or mechanism); Speech power, Speech vagueness (control). Tag: [paper, external]
   Paper's best case: LIWC and TRMI are standard, widely licensed tools, and the paper cites the LIWC manuals and names the vendor database.
   Evidence: LIWC is cited as Pennebaker, Booth, and Francis (2007) and Pennebaker et al. (2007) (p. 15) and is also used for "speech power" (p. 17). `stage-2/model_provenance.md` records that the LIWC2007 manual has no power category and that LIWC2015 lists "Power" among categories "new to LIWC2015," and that LIWC2015 also revised the emotion lists. TRMI is "proprietary" (p. 15). `stage-2/model_provenance.md` records a 25 June 2012 launch release that describes "meaning-generation" scoring of "news and social media," which differs from the paper's "dictionary approach" on "business news media sources" (pp. 15-16). The paper uses daily values from 1998 (p. 12). The vagueness measure uses "a partial dictionary" (p. 17) with no word list. No data or code statement appears in the Methods or table notes (pp. 12-18, 54-57).
   Excerpt: "I was granted full access to the Thomson Reuters Market Psych Indices proprietary database, which calculates a daily measure of the relative level of fear expressed in the United States business media."
   Page: 15
   Severity: Minor
   What would settle it: State the LIWC version and dictionary file used for each LIWC category. State the TRMI asset code, feed (news, social, or combined), index version, and download date, and whether pre-2012 values are backfilled. List the vagueness words used. Archive the per-speech scores and the daily fear series, or say what license terms prevent it.

## Joint rule
None. No generated variable has the role main treatment or main outcome (fingerprint `Generated variables:`; no user corrections in `brief.md`).

## Checked and fine
- Inputs: `stage-1/computational_numbers.md` and `stage-2/model_provenance.md` were both present. `stage-1/dropped_flags.md` lists no dropped flags. Computational flag 1 (no validation of the TRMI fear index) is a measurement-validity question and is not built on here.
- C6, all dictionary variables: the word lists and formula are fixed before estimation and are not fitted to these speeches or to the VIX, so there is no discovery-on-the-estimation-sample problem or train/test leakage (pp. 15-17). The inductively derived speech topics (p. 17) are human codes used as fixed effects, outside this agent's scope.
- C13, text controls (speech uncertainty, negative tone, power, abstractness, vagueness, complexity): each is measured from the prepared speech text, which the interviews place one to six months before delivery (pp. 21-22), so none is measured after the outcome. Whether they share text with BR in the way Issue 2 describes for tone is a lesser concern because they enter only as main-effect controls.
- C10, C11, C12: not applicable. The paper uses no SHAP, feature importance, LASSO selection, double machine learning, or causal forest (methods pp. 12-18; robustness pp. 24-25).
