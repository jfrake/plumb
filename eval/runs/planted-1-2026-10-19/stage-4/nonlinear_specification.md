# Nonlinear specification

## Issues
1. The financial-crisis reversal comes from a 48-speech subsample defined by the H2 and H3 moderators (high positive tone, low media fear) but estimated with Model 2, which leaves out the BR * tone and BR * fear terms. The reported reversal therefore mixes the crisis condition with moderation the paper already attributes to tone and fear, and the specification cannot separate the two.
   Check: N2. Term: BR * financial crisis period dummy, and split sample of crisis speeches with high positive tone and low media fear (N = 48) (subsample comparison, other hypothesis). Tag: [judgment]
   Paper's best case: The paper frames the crisis analysis as a tentative extension ("tentatively extend this story", "preliminary evidence", pp. 19-20), and the pooled BR * crisis interaction in the full sample (beta = -0.051, p = .088 with year fixed effects, p. 20) is a direct test of the crisis difference that does not rest on the 48-speech split.
   Evidence: The split keeps crisis speeches "that contained high positive tone (i.e., above the mean) and low business media fear (i.e., below the mean)" and re-runs "Model 2" (p. 21). Table 2 Model 2 has no BR * Speech positive tone or BR * Business media fear term (p. 56). Under the paper's own Model 3 and Model 5, the BR slope already falls as tone rises (BR * Speech positive tone = -0.056 and -0.049), and the text says that at +1 SD of tone the BR effect "reduces the expected range by 0.5 percent" (p. 19). Under Models 4 and 5 the BR slope also falls as fear drops (BR * Business media fear = 30.590 and 26.293), and the text says that at -1 SD of fear the effect "goes away entirely" (p. 19). A negative BR slope in a high-tone, low-fear cell is thus what the full-sample interactions point toward, crisis or not. The paper reports no high-tone, low-fear subsample outside the crisis period and no model that adds BR * crisis to Model 5. Separately, Table 2 reports df = 58 for Model 2 (p. 57), with year, topic, and local-audience fixed effects plus 22 listed controls; the paper does not say which of these terms were kept when Model 2 was run on 48 speeches (p. 21). Places searched: pp. 19-21, the Robustness Checks section (pp. 24-25), the Figure 6 caption (p. 52), and the Table 2 note (p. 57). Stage 1 built no flag here; its `crisis48-vs-full` record was not computable because the covariance between the subsample and full-sample estimates is not reported (nonlinear_numbers.md, Not computed). Whether that comparison is tested is the nonlinear claims agent's N6, so this entry is limited to what the specification can separate.
   Excerpt: "I ran Model 2 again on this sample and, despite only containing 48 speeches, I found that the main effect entirely reversed (β = -0.155, σ2 = 0.063, p = .022, adjusted R-squared = 0.363) (see Figure 6)."
   Page: 21
   Severity: Weakens a claim
   What would settle it: Estimate the crisis difference in the full sample with Model 5 plus BR * crisis, so the crisis term is net of the BR * tone and BR * fear terms. Or report the same high-tone, low-fear split for non-crisis speeches next to the crisis split. State which controls and fixed effects stay in the 48-speech model. Alternatively, narrow the Discussion's reading (p. 26: "additional validation of this story") to the pooled BR * crisis interaction.

2. Both hypothesized interactions use a single linear product term with a continuous moderator, and the paper reports no flexible check that the BR slope changes linearly with tone or fear. It also includes no squared terms for BR and media fear, even though the two are correlated (r = -0.23).
   Check: N12. Term: BR * Speech positive tone (interaction, other hypothesis H2); BR * Business media fear (interaction, other hypothesis H3). Tag: [paper]
   Paper's best case: H2 and H3 claim only a direction ("suppress", "amplify", pp. 11-12), and a product term with the predicted sign, plotted at plus and minus 1 SD of the moderator (Figures 4 and 5, pp. 51-52), is the conventional test of that directional claim.
   Evidence: The text reads the product terms at plus and minus 1 SD and reports a sign change in the BR effect at each end: at +1 SD of tone the effect "reduces the expected range by 0.5 percent", and at -1 SD of fear it "goes away entirely" (p. 19). With a single product term those end values follow from the linear form. They are not separately estimated. Table 1 reports that BR correlates -0.23 with business media fear (p. 54; the note marks |r| above 0.14 as significant at 0.01), and -0.08 with speech positive tone. No model includes BR squared or fear squared (Table 2, p. 56). I found no binned, tercile, spline, or kernel estimate of either interaction after searching the methods (pp. 12-17), the results (pp. 18-21), the Robustness Checks (pp. 24-25), the Table 1 and Table 2 notes (pp. 54-57), and the Figure 4 and 5 captions (pp. 51-52). A text search of the full PDF for spline, quartile, tercile, binned, piecewise, curvilinear, nonlinear, and centered returned no hits. Stage 1 could not check the moderator ranges because no minimum or maximum is reported for either moderator (nonlinear_numbers.md, Not computed). That range question belongs to the nonlinear claims agent's N4.
   Excerpt: "Consistent with Hypothesis 2, I find that the positive tone of the speech suppresses the market uncertainty created from the Fed Chair explicitly reaffirming the backing (see Figure 4)."
   Page: 18
   Severity: Minor
   What would settle it: Estimate the BR slope within terciles (or another binning) of speech positive tone and of business media fear, with a binning or kernel estimator for the interaction, and report those slopes next to the linear-interaction predictions. Add BR squared and fear squared to Model 4 and Model 5 and report whether BR * Business media fear changes.

## Checked and fine
- N2, BR * Speech positive tone and BR * Business media fear: H2 and H3 claim a change in the slope of a linear BR effect (pp. 11-12). They do not claim a curve, a turning point, or a change in curvature. The model has the product terms that claim needs (Table 2 Models 3-5, p. 56), and the paper plots predicted lines at low and high moderator values (-1 SD and +1 SD; Figures 4 and 5, pp. 51-52). Model 5 enters both interactions together, so each is estimated net of the other (p. 56). Whether the plotted moderator values lie inside the observed range is left to the nonlinear claims agent's N4.
- N7: no squared term appears in any model (Table 2, p. 56), and the fixed effects are year, topic, and local audience (Table 2 note, p. 57; construction ledger). None is a speech-level unit fixed effect, so the check does not apply. Archival is in `run.json` packs, so the general within-unit variation audit is left to that pack.
- N12, BR * financial crisis period dummy: the moderator is binary, so the interaction needs no linearity check (p. 20). The crisis main effect is a linear combination of the year fixed effects, but the BR * crisis interaction is still identified with year fixed effects in the model (p. 20). The paper's remark that the model "includes both" the dummy and year fixed effects should say the dummy's main effect is absorbed.
- Stage 1 inputs: nonlinear_numbers.md lists no flags and no rounding-sensitive results, and dropped_flags.md lists no dropped flags. The construction ledger and contribution_defense.md were read. The paper's defenses (pp. 21-25) do not address functional form or the crisis-subsample specification.
