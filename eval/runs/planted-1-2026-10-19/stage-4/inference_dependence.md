# Inference dependence

## Issues
1. The Table 2 errors are heteroskedasticity-robust only, and they treat every speech as independent although speeches cluster in calendar time and share market-day shocks that year fixed effects do not absorb.
   Check: I8 (with I5). Estimate: BR, Table 2 Model 2, 0.036 (0.018) (main result, H1); also BR x Speech positive tone, Model 3 (H2) and BR x Business media fear, Model 4 (H3). Tag: [judgment]
   Paper's best case: BR varies speech by speech, the outcome is a one-day log change with little persistence, the paper reports that speeches given within three days of each other differ in BR by about one SD (pp. 23-24), and it re-ran all models with GEE and AR(1) errors across a Chair's speeches (pp. 24-25).
   Evidence: Verified facts. The Table 2 note (p. 57) states "robust standard errors" and names no clustering, and the inference ledger records no cluster level for any model. The outcome ln(VIX_t/VIX_t-1) (p. 18) is one market-wide series, so speeches on the same trading date get the same outcome value. The paper reports "N = 40 pairs" of speeches given within three days of each other (p. 23) but not how many share a trading date. The H3 moderator averages media fear over t-1 to t+1 (p. 16), so speeches up to two days apart share part of that regressor. The fixed effects are year, topic, and audience (p. 57), plus weekday dummies (p. 17). None of them absorbs a shock common to speeches in the same day or week. Inference: residuals of speeches close in time may be correlated, and heteroskedasticity-robust errors do not allow for this. This is a judgment; the paper gives no count of same-date speeches and no residual autocorrelation check. For the main result, the printed digits give |t| of 1.919 to 2.086 (arithmetic.md, stars_vs_se line for BR / Model 2, which separately flags the *** mark; that mark belongs to the claims check). A rise in the standard error of about 17 to 27 percent would move the one-tailed p above 0.05. A rise of about 50 to 63 percent would move it above 0.10, the loosest level the note uses. Whether date-level dependence raises the error that much is not settled here.
   Excerpt: "Results show robust regressions with robust standard errors in parentheses."
   Page: 57
   Severity: Weakens a claim
   What would settle it: Report how many speeches share a trading date and how many fall in the same week. Re-estimate Models 2 to 5 with errors clustered by trading date (or by week), or with HAC errors ordered by calendar date, and report the coefficients and errors beside Table 2.

2. The paper's only dependence check (GEE with AR(1) errors across a Chair's speeches) reports no numbers. It also groups the data into three Chairs and orders speeches by sequence rather than calendar time, so as described it cannot show that the Table 2 errors are adequate.
   Check: I9 (with I3 and I5). Estimate: BR, Table 2 Model 2 (main result, H1); also the H2 and H3 interaction terms in Models 3 and 4. Tag: [paper]
   Paper's best case: The author anticipated non-independence across a Chair's successive speeches and re-ran every model with GEE, following Pfarrer et al. (2010) and Wade et al. (2006). The author reports "strong support for all my hypotheses" (pp. 24-25; contribution_defense.md item 7).
   Evidence: Verified facts. The GEE uses Stata XTGEE, a Gaussian family, an identity link, and an AR(1) structure "in which disturbances from a Chair's prior speech are correlated with the disturbances resulting from the current speech" (pp. 24-25). The sample has three Chairs: 159 Greenspan, 166 Bernanke, and 12 Yellen speeches (p. 12). No table, appendix, or footnote reports GEE coefficients, standard errors, or p-values (searched pp. 12-25, Tables 1-2 and notes on pp. 54-57; numeric_fidelity.md row 57 and Issue 15 also found none). The paper does not say whether the GEE variance is model-based or a robust sandwich. Inference: a sandwich variance with the Chair as the panel rests on three clusters, far below the roughly 30 at which cluster-robust errors become reliable, and the cluster sizes are very unequal (12 against 159 and 166). A model-based AR(1) variance instead relies on the AR(1) structure being right. Ordering by a Chair's speech sequence treats two speeches a day apart and two speeches months apart as equally adjacent. So the check does not speak to the date-level dependence in Issue 1.
   Excerpt: "When doing so, I find strong support for all my hypotheses."
   Page: 25
   Severity: Weakens a claim
   What would settle it: Report the GEE coefficients and standard errors for Models 2 to 5 in a table. State whether the variance is model-based or robust and what the panel and time variables are. If a robust variance by Chair is used, say that three clusters cannot support it and rely instead on the date-level or HAC errors from Issue 1.

3. The 48-speech crisis subsample is said to be estimated with "Model 2," but Table 2 gives Model 2 58 degrees of freedom. The paper does not say which terms were dropped, how many fixed-effect cells hold a single speech, or how many residual degrees of freedom the robust error rests on.
   Check: I7 (with I3's small-sample point). Estimate: BR in the crisis-period, high-tone, low-fear subsample, β = -0.155, σ2 = 0.063, p = .022, N = 48 (not listed under Inference; the paper restates it in the Discussion as "additional validation of this story", p. 26). Tag: [paper]
   Paper's best case: The text frames the subsample as exploratory ("To take this exploration one step further", p. 20), flags its size ("despite only containing 48 speeches", p. 21), and reports a p-value that agrees with its coefficient and σ2 read as a standard error (inference_numbers.md, crisis-n48).
   Evidence: Verified facts. Table 2 reports df = 58 for Model 2 (table_02.csv, df row, Model 2 column), and the text says "I ran Model 2 again on this sample" of 48 speeches (p. 21). Model 2 includes year, topic (nine categories), and local-audience (five categories) fixed effects (p. 17, Table 2 note p. 57) and weekday dummies (p. 17). The crisis window covers five years, 2008 to 2012 (p. 20). Stage 1 recorded the df conflict as a paper fact (inference_numbers.md, Notes). The text reports "σ2", which read literally is a variance (pp. 20-21). Inference: the stated specification cannot be estimated on 48 observations, so some terms must have been dropped or absorbed. With that many fixed-effect cells on 48 speeches, some cells will hold one speech, and heteroskedasticity-robust errors are biased downward when residual degrees of freedom are few. Neither the dropped terms nor the residual degrees of freedom are reported.
   Excerpt: "I ran Model 2 again on this sample and, despite only containing 48 speeches, I found that the main effect entirely reversed (β = -0.155, σ2 = 0.063, p = .022, adjusted R-squared = 0.363) (see Figure 6)."
   Page: 21
   Severity: Weakens a claim
   What would settle it: Report the subsample regression in a table with its full term list, the number of singleton fixed-effect cells dropped, the residual degrees of freedom, and the variance estimator. Use a small-sample robust variance (HC2 or HC3 with t critical values at the residual degrees of freedom) or a permutation test of BR within the subsample.

## Checked and fine
- I1, BR (Model 2) and the H2 and H3 interactions (Models 3-4): the key regressor varies at the speech, and errors computed at the speech level are not below that level (Table 2 note, p. 57; fingerprint Inference lines).
- I4, all Table 2 estimates: BR is a continuous regressor, not a treatment that switches on for some groups, so the few-treated-groups check does not apply.
- I3, Table 2 main models: no clustering is used, so no cluster count is owed. N = 339 is printed in every column (table_02.csv, Observations row, p. 57).
- I5, BR (Model 2): the outcome is a one-day log change rather than a persistent level (p. 18). The paper shows that BR is not persistent across nearby speeches, with an average BR difference of 0.21 across 40 pairs within three days (pp. 23-24). It also anticipated within-Chair serial dependence and ran a GEE AR(1) check (pp. 24-25), although the results are not reported (Issue 2).
- I7, full sample: N is 339 in all five models, with no unexplained drop across columns (table_02.csv, Observations row). The 339 against 337 gap with the text (p. 12) belongs to numeric fidelity.
- I10: BR is coded by hand from paragraphs (pp. 13-14), not estimated in a first step. The dictionary-based moderators and controls are listed under Generated variables and are skipped because the computational overlay owns their errors. The ITCV (p. 24) is a sensitivity bound, not a matching or weighting estimator, so no estimated-score correction applies.
- Exclusions: market-event is not in run.json overlays, and the outcome is a VIX change rather than a CAR (fingerprint), so the Table 2 regressions were read as ordinary regressions. No IV, SC, network, or experiment designs apply. No web fetch was needed: the paper names no package for singleton handling and no few-treated-groups method.
