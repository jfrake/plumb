### Structural Gaps

#### Gap 1: Robustness and endogeneity checks are asserted, not shown
- **What's missing:** Coefficient tables for seven checks the paper reports only as conclusions: GEE with AR1 by Chair, "strong support for all my hypotheses" (pp. 24-25); controls for backing-paragraph features (change discussed, future orientation, position), "consistent results" (p. 25); federal funds rate in place of policy dummies, 2- and 4-day fear windows, 15- and 150-day existing-uncertainty windows, "remained consistent" (p. 25); prior average daily VIX changes over 1, 10, 30 days, "did not change my results" (p. 24); local audience, "does not change my results" (p. 23), although audience fixed effects are already in every Table 2 model (note, p. 57). The pre-window placebo gets one line of statistics (p. 25) and no table. There is no appendix (phase1_summary). [paper]
- **Why it matters:** The Discussion builds on "the robustness of this simple finding" and "I proved ... causes" (p. 25). With a design that rests on selection on observables, these checks are the evidence for that claim. A reader cannot see the BR coefficient, its standard error, or the sample in any of them. The backing-feature controls matter most: they are the paper's only answer to the policy-news alternative (theory_devil_report, Alt 1; MD-1), and with no coefficients the reader cannot tell whether BR keeps its size once change-oriented backing is held fixed. The placebo statistics as printed (beta = .041, p = .017) contradict the sentence they sit in, which a table would resolve.
- **Where it should appear:** A robustness table (or appendix tables) reporting BR, BR x tone, and BR x fear with standard errors and N for each check, plus a placebo table showing the pre-window estimate next to the t0 estimate.
- **Severity:** Weakens a claim
- **Confidence:** High. A table of robustness results is a standard component for an archival paper making a causal claim.
- **Tag:** paper

#### Gap 2: The institution's stability and the proposed mediator are never measured; the outcome stops at t0
- **What's missing:** The research question concerns "the stability of an institution" (p. 1), and the mechanism runs through lost taken-for-grantedness and "greater heterogeneity in overall judgments and decisions made in the market" (p. 9). The only outcome is the log change in VIX from t-1 to t0 (pp. 13, 18). Three components are absent: (a) any measure of judgment dispersion on speech days, such as dispersion of professional forecasts or of fed funds futures-implied paths; (b) any post-speech window (t+1 to t+5 or longer) showing whether the rise persists, which the claim of a "destabilized" institution implies and a transient news jump does not (argument_map, Falsifiability (c)); (c) any evidence tying BR to taken-for-grantedness or cognitive legitimacy apart from its correlation with audience sophistication (pp. 22-23), even though p. 34 proposes BR as "a potential direct measure of cognitive legitimacy." [paper; judgment on what the theory implies]
- **Why it matters:** The contribution the paper claims is institutional (pp. 32-35): backing "jeopardiz[es] the stability of the institution" (p. 33) and is "precisely what our institutions are built upon" (p. 35). What is delivered is a one-day volatility association. The gap between the claim and the evidence is structural, not a matter of identification. A persistence check and a dispersion outcome are the components that would let the evidence reach the claimed level; the argument map derives both as predictions the paper does not state. (Whether these results would discriminate between rival mechanisms is MD-1's question; here the point is that the claimed construct has no measure.)
- **Where it should appear:** Results, after H1: a figure or table of the BR coefficient across post-speech windows, and an analysis with a dispersion outcome. In the Methods, a validation of BR against an independent indicator, or a narrowing of the "direct measure of cognitive legitimacy" claim (p. 34).
- **Severity:** Weakens a claim
- **Confidence:** Medium-High. The persistence window is a low-cost, expected component for an event-style claim about destabilization; the dispersion outcome is a stronger request that reasonable reviewers may treat as optional.
- **Tag:** paper + judgment

#### Gap 3: The scope condition is tested only where it is said not to hold
- **What's missing:** The theory applies "in a context where the underlying institutional assumptions are reasonably stable and taken-for-granted," which the paper says was "generally true across the time period examined" except the 2008-2012 crisis (p. 19). The paper reports the crisis split (BR = 0.035, p = .335, p. 20) and the BR x crisis interaction (p. 20), but not the H1 estimate for the non-crisis years, 1998-2007 and 2013-2014, where the theory claims to apply. The crisis analyses have no table, the crisis split's N is not given, and the 48-speech subsample (high tone and low fear, p. 21) is not compared against the remaining crisis speeches. [paper]
- **Why it matters:** The Discussion presents the crisis result as "additional validation of this story" (p. 26) and builds a contingent theory on it (reaffirmation reinforces when taken-for-grantedness is weak, p. 26). The most direct support for that reading is a positive and larger BR effect outside the crisis, and that estimate is absent. The theory devil report and argument map note that H3 predicts amplification in the high-fear crisis while the paper finds attenuation (argument_map, Internal Consistency); the missing non-crisis estimate is the number a reader needs to judge that tension.
- **Where it should appear:** A table in the Supplemental Analyses section (pp. 19-21) with Model 2 and Model 5 for non-crisis speeches, crisis speeches, and the pooled interaction, each with N.
- **Severity:** Weakens a claim
- **Confidence:** Medium. The analysis is labelled tentative (pp. 19-20), but the Discussion relies on it as validation (p. 26), which raises the bar.
- **Tag:** paper

#### Gap 4: Effect sizes for the headline and the signaling claim are not derivable from what is shown
- **What's missing:** The 1.0, -0.5, +3.5, and +2.5 percent figures (pp. 18-19) give no model, coefficients, or formula. The text uses SD of BR = 0.21 (p. 18); Table 1 reports 0.22 (p. 54). For the signaling contribution, "longer speeches significantly reduce market uncertainty" (p. 28) rests on a word-count coefficient printed as -0.000*** in every model (table_02.csv), with no magnitude in any unit. [paper]
- **Why it matters:** The paper argues practical significance through the VXX trade and hedging cost (p. 18) and offers leaders guidance based on the tone and fear contrasts (p. 30). A reader cannot reproduce any of those numbers. The word-count result is presented as a second contribution (pp. 27-28) with no stated effect size. Arithmetic discrepancies are left to the numeric agents (phase1_summary item 9); the gap here is the missing derivation and the missing magnitude.
- **Where it should appear:** A short footnote or table note after each effect-size sentence (pp. 18-19), and word count rescaled (for example, per 1,000 words) in Table 2.
- **Severity:** Minor
- **Confidence:** Medium
- **Tag:** paper

#### Gap 5: No limitations or external-validity discussion despite general and global claims
- **What's missing:** The paper has no limitations section (contribution_defense, Acknowledged Limitations: "No dedicated limitations section"). It claims effects on "financial markets around the world" from a U.S. equity-options index (pp. 13, 25), a "more general principle" illustrated by a teacher and an airline pilot (pp. 26-27), and guidance for Supreme Court Justices, Presidents, and corporate executives (pp. 29-30). It does not discuss what features of the Fed setting the result depends on: a single speaker with market-moving authority, an audience of professional traders, one outcome measured within a day, and three Chairs with 12 Yellen speeches (p. 12). [paper; judgment on scope]
- **Why it matters:** The generalizations carry the paper's theoretical reach (pp. 26, 29-35). Without a scope discussion, a reader cannot tell which parts of the principle the Fed evidence supports. The "around the world" claim has no evidence in the paper.
- **Where it should appear:** A limitations and boundary-conditions subsection at the end of the Discussion; either evidence on non-U.S. volatility indices or removal of "around the world."
- **Severity:** Minor
- **Confidence:** Medium
- **Tag:** paper + judgment

### Summary
The paper delivers estimates for each stated hypothesis, but it is incomplete relative to its institutional and causal ambition: the evidence stops at a two-day VIX change and leaves the institution, the mediator, and persistence unmeasured. The most important gap is Gap 1: every robustness and endogeneity check behind "I proved ... causes" (p. 25) appears only as a sentence, with no table and no appendix. Five gaps: three Weakens a claim (Gaps 1-3), two Minor (Gaps 4-5), none Undermines the main claim on completeness grounds alone.
