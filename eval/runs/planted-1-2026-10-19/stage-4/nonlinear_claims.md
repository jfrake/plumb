# Nonlinear claims

## Issues
1. The text says that at high positive tone (+1 SD) reaffirming the backing "in fact, reduces" market uncertainty. The paper offers no test of that conditional slope, and the reported standard errors support it only under a strongly negative covariance between the BR and product-term estimates.
   Check: N4. Term: BR * Speech positive tone (interaction, other hypothesis H2). Tag: [paper]
   Paper's best case: H2 predicts only that positive tone weakens the BR effect, and the product term (-0.056, SE 0.020, Model 3) together with Figure 4 shows that weakening. The reversal is offered as an illustration of the plotted point estimates.
   Evidence: Table 2 Model 3 (p. 56): BR 0.034 (0.018), BR * Speech positive tone -0.056 (0.020); Table 1 (p. 54): positive tone SD 1.01. Figure 4's lines (p. 51) match a mean-centered moderator: the -1 SD line rises about 0.09 over BR 0 to 1 and the +1 SD line falls about 0.023. Computed from the printed numbers (inference, assuming tone was centered as Figure 4 implies): slope at +1 SD = 0.034 - 1.01 x 0.056 = -0.023. With zero covariance its z is -0.83. It reaches the paper's one-tailed .10 level only if the correlation between the two estimates is -0.58 or lower, and the .05 level only at -0.75 or lower. The covariance is not reported (Stage 1, nonlinear_numbers.md, "Not computed"). In Model 5 the same slope is 0.041 - 1.01 x 0.049 = -0.009, closer to zero. Neither Figure 4 nor Figure 5 shows confidence bands, and no Johnson-Neyman region or simple-slope test appears in the results (pp. 18-19), the robustness section (pp. 24-25), the figure captions (pp. 51-52), or the table notes (p. 57). The Discussion then builds practical advice on the reversal: leaders "can ensure that discussing them does not create uncertainty by either encasing these statements within more positive language or postponing the discussion until a prevailing pessimistic climate passes" (p. 30). The magnitude the text gives at -1 SD tone (3.5 percent) does not match Table 2 or Figure 4. Stage 1 already records this as numeric_fidelity.md Issue 7 (1.9 percent by the paper's own method), so it is not restated here. By contrast, the slope at -1 SD (0.091) is distinguishable from zero at the one-tailed .05 level under every covariance the standard errors allow (minimum z = 2.37).
   Excerpt: "This means that when a speech’s positive tone is one standard deviation above its mean, the aforementioned 1.0 percent increase in the expected range of the S&P 500 over the next month goes away and, in fact, reduces the expected range by 0.5 percent."
   Page: 18-19
   Severity: Weakens a claim
   What would settle it: Report the conditional effect of BR at +1 SD (and across the observed range) of positive tone with its standard error or interval, using the estimated covariance, for Models 3 and 5. Or narrow the text to say that the effect is attenuated at high tone, and drop the "reduces" reading and the advice on p. 30 that depends on it.

2. The claim that, among 48 crisis-period speeches with high positive tone and low media fear, reaffirming the backing "actually decreases uncertainty" rests on a single post hoc subsample estimate. The paper reports no test of its difference from the full-sample effect, and the model as described has almost no residual degrees of freedom at N = 48.
   Check: N6. Term: BR in crisis, high-tone, low-fear subsample, N = 48 (subsample comparison, other hypothesis; fingerprint lists it with the crisis analysis). Tag: [paper]
   Paper's best case: The analysis is framed as exploratory ("To take this exploration one step further", p. 20), and the subsample estimate itself is negative with a reported p = .022.
   Evidence: Text p. 21: beta = -0.155, "σ2 = 0.063", p = .022, adjusted R-squared = 0.363; Figure 6 (p. 52). Stage 1 record crisis48-vs-full returned not_computable: the 48 speeches are a subset of the 339, and no covariance, pooled interaction, or test of equal coefficients is reported. The cell is defined by splitting both hypothesized moderators at their means inside a period chosen after the main analysis. The paper says it "ran Model 2 again on this sample". Table 2 lists df = 58 for Model 2 (p. 56). Inference: even after the year fixed effects shrink to the five crisis years, a model of that size leaves at most a few residual degrees of freedom with 48 observations, unless terms were dropped. The paper does not say which terms were estimated. If "σ2" is a standard error, -0.155/0.063 gives z = -2.46, which is two-tailed p = .014 under a normal reference. The printed .022 is consistent with a t reference on few degrees of freedom, but the paper states neither the reference distribution nor which terms remain. The p. 30 advice ("encasing these statements within more positive language") leans on this result together with issue 1.
   Excerpt: "What this suggests is that, when the taken-for-grantedness of the prevailing assumptions has been weakened and the emotional context is highly optimistic, explicitly reaffirming these assumptions actually decreases uncertainty."
   Page: 21
   Severity: Weakens a claim
   What would settle it: Estimate the pattern in the full sample with a pooled specification (BR x crisis x tone and BR x crisis x fear, or BR x crisis interacted with a high-tone/low-fear indicator), and report the conditional slope in that cell with its interval. Report which terms the N = 48 model estimates and its residual degrees of freedom. Otherwise describe the result as a descriptive pattern in one post hoc cell.

3. The claim that the BR effect weakened during the financial crisis rests on a pooled interaction that is marginal (p = .088) under what appears to be a one-tailed test for a non-hypothesized term. The crisis-only split gives a point estimate equal to the full-sample one, yet the paper reads it as the effect dropping. The Discussion then restates the result as "validation" of the theory.
   Check: N6. Term: BR * financial crisis period dummy; crisis-only split sample (subsample comparison, other hypothesis). Tag: [paper]
   Paper's best case: The paper does run the right kind of test, a pooled BR x crisis interaction rather than only comparing significance across splits. It calls the evidence "preliminary" and the extension "tentative" (pp. 19-20).
   Evidence: Text p. 20: interaction beta = -0.051, "σ2 = 0.038", p = .088 with year fixed effects; beta = -0.071, σ2 = 0.036, p = .026 without them. Crisis-only split: beta = 0.035, σ2 = 0.036, p = .335, against the full-sample 0.036 (SE 0.018; Table 2 Model 2). Reading σ2 as a standard error: -0.051/0.038 gives z = -1.34, one-tailed p = .090 and two-tailed p = .18. 0.035/0.036 gives z = 0.97, two-tailed p = .33. So the printed p for the interaction matches a one-tailed test, while the printed p for the split matches a two-tailed test. The crisis interaction is not a numbered hypothesis, and Table 2's note restricts one-tailed tests to "directional hypotheses" (p. 57). The split's point estimate shows no weakening. Only its precision differs, and the text reads the loss of significance as the effect weakening ("the main effect results for H1 drop below significance ... This provides preliminary evidence", p. 20). The label "σ2" (a variance, read literally) is used for all four of these estimates (Stage 1, nonlinear_numbers.md, "Terms checked").
   Excerpt: "Interestingly, as additional validation of this story, I also illustrated how, during the financial crisis, when the taken-for-grantedness of these assumptions had weakened, reaffirming them no longer creates uncertainty to the same extent."
   Page: 26
   Severity: Weakens a claim
   What would settle it: Report the pooled BR x crisis estimate in a table with its standard error (labeled as such), state the tails used, and report the BR slope in crisis and non-crisis years with intervals. Or keep the Discussion's wording at the level of the p. 20 text ("preliminary evidence") rather than "validation".

4. The paper reports neither the observed range of either moderator nor whether the moderators were centered, and it prints the SD of business media fear as 0.00. A reader cannot confirm that the plotted plus and minus 1 SD values lie inside the data or recompute the H3 conditional effects.
   Check: N4. Term: BR * Speech positive tone (interaction, other hypothesis H2); BR * Business media fear (interaction, other hypothesis H3). Tag: [paper]
   Paper's best case: Plotting at the mean plus and minus 1 SD is the field's conventional display, and Figures 4 and 5 agree with the Table 2 coefficients if the moderators were centered.
   Evidence: Table 1 (p. 54) gives positive tone mean 3.42, SD 1.01 and business media fear mean 0.0084, SD "0.00". No minimum, maximum, or percentile for either moderator appears in Table 1, the measures section (pp. 15-16), the results (pp. 18-19), the figure captions (pp. 51-52), or the table notes (pp. 55, 57) (Stage 1, nonlinear_numbers.md, "Not computed"). The paper never states centering (construction_ledger.md, Speech positive tone caveats). Without centering, the BR coefficient in Models 3 to 5 is the effect at tone = 0 or fear = 0, which is likely outside the data for tone. Only Figure 4's agreement with a centered computation indicates which reading applies. Figure 5's slopes (about 0.09 at +1 SD and near zero at -1 SD) imply a fear SD near 0.0015. That is an inference from the figure, not a printed number. Stage 1 numeric_fidelity.md Issue 8 covers the H3 magnitude (2.5 percent) separately.
   Excerpt: "Consistent with Hypothesis 3, I find that business media fear present at the time of the speech amplifies the market uncertainty created from the Fed Chair explicitly reaffirming the backing (see Figure 5)."
   Page: 19
   Severity: Minor
   What would settle it: State whether each moderator was centered (or standardized) before forming the product. Report the minimum and maximum of each moderator, and print the SD of business media fear with enough digits (or rescale the variable).

5. The paper gives the H2 and H3 conditional effects in outcome units at plus and minus 1 SD of each moderator, but with no standard errors or intervals.
   Check: N13. Term: BR * Speech positive tone (interaction, other hypothesis H2); BR * Business media fear (interaction, other hypothesis H3). Tag: [paper]
   Paper's best case: The paper does translate both interactions into outcome units at stated moderator values (pp. 18-19), which many papers omit.
   Evidence: Effect sizes are stated as "0.5 percent", "3.5 percent", "2.5 percent", and "goes away entirely" (pp. 18-19) with no uncertainty. Figures 4 and 5 (pp. 51-52) plot predicted values without bands. For the high-tone effect this gap carries the most weight (issue 1).
   Excerpt: "Consistent with Hypothesis 2, I find that the positive tone of the speech suppresses the market uncertainty created from the Fed Chair explicitly reaffirming the backing (see Figure 4)."
   Page: 18
   Severity: Minor
   What would settle it: Report each conditional effect with its standard error or confidence interval, and add confidence bands to Figures 4 and 5.

## Checked and fine
- N3, BR * Speech positive tone and BR * Business media fear: every constitutive term is in Models 3 to 5 (Table 2, p. 56). No sentence reads the BR coefficient in Models 3 to 5 as "the effect of BR"; the text reads conditional effects at plus and minus 1 SD (pp. 18-19). Stage 1 records the anomalous lower-order fear coefficient in Model 4 (0.005** with SE 2.924) as numeric_fidelity.md Issue 3, so it is not restated here.
- N3, BR * financial crisis dummy: the crisis dummy covers whole calendar years 2008 to 2012, so the year fixed effects absorb it. The constitutive term is therefore included, which also makes the paper's note that the model "includes both the financial crisis period dummy variable, along with year fixed-effects" (p. 20) redundant rather than wrong.
- N4, BR * Speech positive tone at -1 SD: the slope (0.091 per unit BR; Figure 4, p. 51) is distinguishable from zero at the paper's one-tailed .05 level under every covariance the reported standard errors allow (minimum z = 2.37).
- N4, H2 and H3 direction: each product term's sign matches the hypothesized shape (weakens for H2, strengthens for H3; hypothesis_ledger.json). Each hypothesis is offered with a figure across the moderator, not with the product term alone (Figures 4 and 5, pp. 51-52).
- N5: the paper reports average model VIF (Table 2, p. 57) but does not present centering as a remedy for multicollinearity and does not blame a weak interaction on multicollinearity.
- N6, H2 and H3: moderation is tested by pooled product terms in the full sample, not by comparing split samples (Table 2 Models 3 to 5).
- N1, N8, N9, N10, N11: not applicable. The paper has no squared terms. All models are linear (OLS or robust regression; Table 2 note, p. 57), so there are no odds, rate, or hazard ratios. The outcome ln(VIX_t / VIX_t-1) is a log ratio of strictly positive index levels, not a log-like transform of an outcome with zeros (p. 18). There are no three-way interactions.
- Stage 1 inputs: nonlinear_numbers.md reported no flags and no rounding-sensitive results; dropped_flags.md lists none.
