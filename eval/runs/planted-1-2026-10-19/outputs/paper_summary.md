# Paper Summary

**Title:** Archival Test Paper 1 (p. 1; marked "PLUMB TEST COPY: ALTERED FROM AN ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT")
**Authors:** Not listed on the manuscript (single author writing in the first person, e.g., p. 2)
**Venue/Status:** Not stated. The header describes it as an altered copy of an accepted manuscript (p. 1).

## Research Question

What happens to the stability of an institution when a prominent leader explicitly reaffirms its taken-for-granted assumptions (p. 1)? The paper also asks how speech positive tone and business media fear moderate that effect (p. 3).

## Setting and Data

The setting is speeches by Federal Reserve Chairs (pp. 3, 12). The initial sample is all Chair speeches from January 1, 1998 to December 31, 2014 (344 speeches). Seven outliers with studentized residuals beyond plus or minus three were removed, leaving 337 speeches: 159 by Greenspan, 166 by Bernanke, and 12 by Yellen (p. 12). Table 1's note and Table 2 report 339 speeches/observations (pp. 55, 57). The unit is the speech. The outcome is market uncertainty, measured as the change in the VIX over a two-day window (t-1 to t0) (p. 13). The treatment is the Backing Ratio (BR): paragraphs that make the backing explicit divided by total paragraphs. The author and three undergraduates hand-coded the paragraphs (Krippendorff's alpha 0.88 and .84) (pp. 13-14). The moderators are LIWC positive tone (p. 15) and a Thomson Reuters MarketPsych business media fear index averaged over t-1 to t+1 (pp. 15-16). Controls cover market conditions, same-day releases, speech characteristics, weekday, location, nine topics, and five audiences (pp. 16-17).

## Method

The design is an event study estimated by OLS with year fixed effects, using ln(VIX_t/VIX_t-1) as the dependent variable (p. 18). Table 2's note describes robust regressions with robust standard errors, one-tailed tests for directional hypotheses, and year, topic, and local-audience fixed effects (p. 57). The author addresses endogeneity with controls and with interviews of Bernanke, Kohn, and Olson about speechwriting timing, reporting that speeches are scheduled about six months ahead and rarely changed in the final week (pp. 21-22). Other checks include correlations of BR with audience type (p. 22), BR differences among speeches given within three days of each other (N = 40 pairs) (p. 23), lagged VIX changes as controls (p. 24), an ITCV test (p. 24), GEE with AR(1) errors (pp. 24-25), additional backing codings, a pre-speech placebo window, and alternative control definitions (p. 25). Supplemental analyses cover the 2008-2012 financial crisis period (pp. 19-21).

## Headline Findings

- H1: BR raises market uncertainty. A one-SD increase in BR (0.21) raises the expected S&P 500 range by 1.0 percent (p. 18). The Model 2 BR coefficient is 0.036*** (0.018) (p. 56).
- H2: Positive tone suppresses the effect. At +1 SD tone the effect reverses to -0.5 percent; at -1 SD it rises to 3.5 percent (pp. 18-19). The BR x tone coefficient is -0.056*** in Model 3 (p. 56).
- H3: Media fear amplifies the effect by 2.5 percent at +1 SD, and the effect disappears at -1 SD (p. 19). The BR x fear coefficient is 30.590*** in Model 4 (p. 56).
- Financial crisis: the BR x crisis interaction is β = -0.051, p = .088. The crisis-only BR effect is β = 0.035, p = .335. In the subsample of 48 high-tone, low-fear crisis speeches, β = -0.155, p = .022 (pp. 20-21).
- Placebo: for the pre-speech window, β = .041, p = .017 (p. 25).

## Claimed Contribution

The author maps Toulmin's "backing" onto taken-for-granted assumptions. On that basis the paper claims that explicitly reaffirming these assumptions destabilizes the institution, which contributes to work on the micro-foundations of institutions (pp. 2-3, 32-34). Further claimed contributions: the backing ratio as a measure of cognitive legitimacy (p. 34), emotions treated as an interpretive context rather than a signal, a complement to signaling theory (pp. 27-29), and implications for transparency (pp. 29-32).
